\begin{abstract}
In limited-angle tomographic reconstruction, weighted data-fidelity terms cause low spatial-frequency image components to converge slowly. The square-root Hanning weighting that de-emphasizes near-DC data inconsistencies for noise robustness also collapses the corresponding singular values of the forward operator, while the global Chambolle--Pock step-size constraint $\tau\sigma\|K\|^2<1$ forces the step sizes to be dictated by the well-conditioned high-frequency band; the modes needing the strongest correction receive the weakest. Splitting the sinogram residual into complementary low- and high-pass channels, each with its own $\ell_2$ constraint and its own dual step size, allows the near-DC band to be corrected independently of the rest of the spectrum. Across five phantoms in limited-angle fan-beam geometry, amplifying the low-frequency dual step by a factor of four lowers the image RMSE at a fixed iteration budget by $9$ to $36\%$ relative to a single-channel baseline, with the largest gains on the phantoms carrying the most low-frequency content. The amplification factor is not tuned but certified: recasting the channel step sizes as a diagonal preconditioner reduces the admissible range to a single computable bound, $\tau\,\lambda_{\max}(\sum_j\sigma_jK_j^\top K_j)<1$, evaluated by one power iteration. This bound is strictly sharper than the worst-channel surrogate, and the spectral separation it exploits is measurable on the reconstruction operator itself. The same analysis extends to a \emph{dyadic} multi-band fidelity bank in which the residual is partitioned into octave-localized channels; with a geometric dual-step schedule of base $2$, the stability bound is independent of the number of bands, so the near-DC dual can be amplified across many octaves at no cost in the primal step.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Limited angular range (LAR) acquisition arises whenever the source cannot traverse a full arc around the object, either because the geometry forbids it or because dose and acquisition time are constrained. Digital breast tomosynthesis (DBT) is the canonical clinical instance, with the source restricted to an arc of typically $15^\circ$ to $50^\circ$, but the same regime occurs in dental and intraoral tomosynthesis, chest tomosynthesis, image-guided intervention, and non-destructive testing. In each case the resulting linear system is severely ill-conditioned and, at useful isotropic discretizations, underdetermined. Stable inversion at these arcs became possible only once discrete-to-discrete image models were paired with sparsity-promoting convex regularization. Building on the observation that sparsity of directional derivative magnitudes improves LAR recovery, \citet{zhang2021directional} introduced a globally convex framework based on directional total variation (DTV), and \citet{sidky2025accurate} developed a data-discrepancy-constrained, DTV- and pixel-sparsity-regularized model for DBT that is solved with a primal--dual hybrid gradient (PDHG / Chambolle--Pock) method~\citep{chambolle2011first} accelerated by He--Yuan relaxation~\citep{he2012convergence}.

Low spatial-frequency components converge slowly in this setting. The model of \citet{sidky2025accurate} measures data discrepancy through a square-root Hanning (Hann$^{1/2}$) filter applied along detector channels; the filter de-emphasizes low-frequency inconsistencies for noise robustness. In the detector-frequency domain this weighting attenuates near-DC modes, shrinking the already-small low-frequency singular values of the limited-angle forward operator. Because the largest singular values lie in the mid- and high-frequency bands, the global PDHG stability condition $\tau\sigma\|K\|^2<1$ ties the admissible step sizes to those bands, and the near-DC modes receive only weak per-iteration correction. High- and mid-band components converge quickly, while the low-band error decays slowly and dominates the tail of the iteration.

A natural remedy is to split the data-consistency term into two complementary spectral channels, a high/band-pass channel that preserves the original weighting and a low-pass channel supported near DC~\citep{iyadomi2026accelerating}, each enforced by its own $\ell_2$ constraint and each driven by its own dual step size. Assigning a larger step to the low-pass channel supplies a stronger correction to the near-DC band. The scalar theory does not govern this construction: the Chambolle--Pock theorem assumes a \emph{single} dual step size $\sigma$, and the condition $\tau\sigma\|K\|^2<1$ does not accommodate distinct $\sigma_{\mathrm{hi}}$ and $\sigma_{\mathrm{lo}}$. It therefore neither certifies the two-channel iteration nor bounds the admissible low-frequency step, and the amplification factor is left as a tuning parameter.

General convergence theory for this situation is available: \citet{pock2011diagonal} prove that the primal--dual iteration converges for symmetric positive definite preconditioners $T$ and $\Sigma$ under the weighted condition $\|\Sigma^{1/2}KT^{1/2}\|<1$, and the per-channel step sizes are exactly a diagonal $\Sigma$. What that condition does not supply is a way to evaluate it or a rule for setting the steps. Our contribution is to make it operational for the split-fidelity operator. Theorem~\ref{thm:main} collapses the weighted operator norm, for $T=\tau I$ and block-diagonal $\Sigma$, to the single computable bound $\tau\,\lambda_{\max}(\sum_j\sigma_jK_j^\top K_j)<1$, evaluated by one power iteration on a weighted normal operator that is never assembled, and writes the resulting bounds out per channel so that the effect of amplifying the low-frequency step is explicit. Remark~\ref{rmk:tight} shows this bound is strictly sharper than the conservative worst-channel rule, and identifies the spectral band separation that governs the size of the improvement, a property measurable on the reconstruction operator itself. Remark~\ref{rmk:choose} and Theorem~\ref{thm:two-channel} turn the bound into a step-size selection procedure with an exact ceiling on the low-frequency dual. Section~\ref{sec:experiments} evaluates the resulting method on five phantoms. Finally, we generalize the two-channel split to a \emph{dyadic} multi-band fidelity bank (Section~\ref{sec:dyadic}) and show that, under a geometric dual-step schedule of base $2$ matched to the per-octave energy decay, the stability bound is independent of the number of bands.

\section{Preliminaries}
\label{sec:prelim}

\subsection{Imaging model and directional total variation}

We consider LAR DBT in a discrete-to-discrete setting. Let $f\in\R^{n}$ denote the discretized (nonnegative) attenuation image, $g\in\R^{m}$ the measured sinogram, and $X\in\R^{m\times n}$ the system matrix encoding the fan-beam projection. The idealized data model is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:fwd}
g = X f .
\end{equation}
Let $\partial_x,\partial_y,\partial_z$ be finite-difference operators approximating the partial derivatives along the corresponding axes, with associated DTV seminorms $\|\partial_x f\|_1,\|\partial_y f\|_1,\|\partial_z f\|_1$ (in 2D the $\partial_y$ term is omitted). Distinguishing in-plane from depth derivatives is important in LAR/DBT because data support and conditioning are direction-dependent.

\subsection{Two-channel constrained reconstruction}

The single-channel model of \citet{sidky2025accurate} minimizes a sparsity-regularized objective subject to a filtered data-discrepancy constraint,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:single}
\min_{f\ge 0}\;
\alpha_x\|\partial_x f\|_1+\alpha_y\|\partial_y f\|_1+\alpha_z\|\partial_z f\|_1+\beta\|f\|_1
\quad\text{s.t.}\quad
\big\|R[c]\,(g-Xf)\big\|_2\le\varepsilon\sqrt{m},
\end{equation}
where $R[c]=\Lambda^{1/2}\,\mathrm{Hann}^{1/2}(c)$ acts along detector channels: $\Lambda^{1/2}$ is the square-root ramp (the Fourier multiplier $|u|^{1/2}$) and $\mathrm{Hann}^{1/2}(c)$ a square-root Hanning window of cutoff parameter $c$. We use throughout the convention that $c$ sets the reciprocal half-width of the window, so that $\mathrm{Hann}(c)$ is supported on $|u|\le\Omega/c$ with $\Omega$ the largest detector frequency; \emph{larger} $c$ therefore means a \emph{narrower} window concentrated closer to DC. The ramp preconditions the fidelity by flattening the $1/|u|$ spectral decay of $X^\top X$, and both factors vanish at DC, so $R[c]^\top R[c]=X^\top\big(|u|\,\mathrm{Hann}(c)\big)X$ suppresses the low-frequency singular values. This de-emphasis slows the convergence of near-DC image content, but it also separates the near-DC band spectrally from the high-frequency band, the property the band-separation argument of Remark~\ref{rmk:tight} rests on.

To retain this robustness while applying a stronger correction to the low band, we split the data constraint into two complementary spectral channels:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:two}
\min_{f\ge 0}\;
\alpha_x\|\partial_x f\|_1+\alpha_y\|\partial_y f\|_1+\alpha_z\|\partial_z f\|_1+\beta\|f\|_1
\quad\text{s.t.}\quad
\begin{cases}
\|R_{\mathrm{hi}}(g-Xf)\|_2\le\varepsilon_{\mathrm{hi}}\sqrt{m},\\[0.25em]
\|R_{\mathrm{lo}}(g-Xf)\|_2\le\varepsilon_{\mathrm{lo}}\sqrt{m}.
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
Both filters carry the same square-root ramp as the single-channel fidelity and differ only in their Hanning window. The low channel is the narrow near-DC window
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:Rlo}
R_{\mathrm{lo}}:=\Lambda^{1/2}\mathrm{Hann}^{1/2}(c_{\mathrm{lo}}),
\qquad c_{\mathrm{lo}}\gg c,
\end{equation}
and the high channel is defined as its complement within the original weighting,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:Rhi}
R_{\mathrm{hi}}^\top R_{\mathrm{hi}}
:=|u|\big(\mathrm{Hann}(c)-\mathrm{Hann}(c_{\mathrm{lo}})\big),
\end{equation}
which is nonnegative because $\mathrm{Hann}(c_{\mathrm{lo}})\le\mathrm{Hann}(c)$ pointwise for $c_{\mathrm{lo}}\ge c$. The two channels then partition the single-channel weighting exactly,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:two-channel-partition}
R_{\mathrm{hi}}^\top R_{\mathrm{hi}}+R_{\mathrm{lo}}^\top R_{\mathrm{lo}}=R[c]^\top R[c],
\end{equation}
so the split changes only the preconditioning of the iteration, not the data-fidelity content. This is the depth-$2$ instance of the dyadic bank constructed in Section~\ref{sec:dyadic}; defining $R_{\mathrm{hi}}$ instead as the full window $\Lambda^{1/2}\mathrm{Hann}^{1/2}(c)$ would leave the two channels overlapping near DC and \eqref{eq:two-channel-partition} would fail. The shared ramp is essential to the construction: by suppressing the near-DC singular values, it keeps the low band's contribution to the weighted operator norm small. This is the band separation that Remark~\ref{rmk:tight} relies on; without it the low and high bands overlap, the separation is lost, and the certified low-frequency amplification no longer provides a benefit. The low-frequency tolerance is chosen slightly looser, $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{lo}}>\varepsilon_{\mathrm{hi}}$, to permit mild model mismatch at DC while still reducing large-scale bias.

\subsection{Saddle-point form and the PDHG algorithm}

This section recasts the constrained problem \eqref{eq:two} as a convex--concave saddle-point problem, the form to which the primal--dual machinery of \citet{chambolle2011first} applies, and records the two structural properties of that form on which the convergence analysis of Section~\ref{sec:main} depends.

Stack the regularization and forward operators into a single linear map and collect the dual variables blockwise:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:K}
K=\begin{bmatrix} R_{\mathrm{hi}}X\\ R_{\mathrm{lo}}X\\ \partial_x\\ \partial_y\\ \partial_z\end{bmatrix},
\qquad
y=\big(y_{\mathrm{hi}},\,y_{\mathrm{lo}},\,p_x,\,p_y,\,p_z\big).
\end{equation}
Then \eqref{eq:two} is an instance of the convex--concave saddle problem
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:saddle}
\min_{x}\;\max_{y}\;\; \Phi(x,y):=\langle Kx,y\rangle+G(x)-F^\ast(y),
\end{equation}
where $G(x)=\beta\|x\|_1+\iota_{\{x\ge0\}}(x)$ gathers pixel sparsity and nonnegativity, and $F^\ast(y)=\sum_j F_j^\ast(y_j)$ is \emph{separable across blocks}: for each data channel $c\in\{\mathrm{hi},\mathrm{lo}\}$, $F_c^\ast(y_c)=\langle y_c,R_c g\rangle+\varepsilon_c\sqrt{m}\,\|y_c\|_2$ is the support function of the $\ell_2$ ball (so its resolvent is a shift by the data followed by projection onto $\{\|u\|_2\le\varepsilon_c\sqrt m\}$), and for each TV channel $d\in\{x,y,z\}$, $F_d^\ast(p_d)=\iota_{\{\|\cdot\|_\infty\le\alpha_d\}}(p_d)$ (so its resolvent is projection onto the $\ell_\infty$ ball). The two structural facts that the convergence analysis uses are that $K$ is block-stacked and $F^\ast$ is block-separable.

Preconditioned PDHG assigns each block its own dual step size through a diagonal dual preconditioner $\Sigma$ and a primal preconditioner $T$:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:precond-def}
T=\tau I_n,\qquad
\Sigma=\diag\!\big(\sigma_{\mathrm{hi}}I,\;\sigma_{\mathrm{lo}}I,\;\sigma_x I,\;\sigma_y I,\;\sigma_z I\big).
\end{equation}
Because $\Sigma$ is block-diagonal and $F^\ast$ separable, the dual update decouples into one proximal step per block, each at its own rate, while the scalar $T=\tau I$ keeps the primal proximal step (soft-thresholding plus nonnegativity clamp) separable. Algorithm~\ref{alg:two} states the iteration; it is exactly the two-channel method, with the data duals projected onto their $\ell_2$ balls at rates $\sigma_{\mathrm{hi}},\sigma_{\mathrm{lo}}$ and the TV duals onto their $\ell_\infty$ balls.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Preconditioned PDHG with split low/high fidelity channels}
\label{alg:two}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\State \textbf{Input:} $g,X,R_{\mathrm{hi}},R_{\mathrm{lo}},\alpha_{x,y,z},\beta,\varepsilon_{\mathrm{hi}},\varepsilon_{\mathrm{lo}}$; steps $\tau,\sigma_{\mathrm{hi}},\sigma_{\mathrm{lo}},\sigma_{x,y,z}>0$ with $\tau\lambda_{\max}(M)<1$ (Thm~\ref{thm:main}); extrapolation $\theta=1$.
\State \textbf{Initialize:} $f^{-1}=f^{0}=0$, all duals $=0$.
\For{$n=0,1,2,\dots,N-1$}
  \State $\bar f^{\,n}\gets f^{n}+\theta\,(f^{n}-f^{n-1})$ \Comment{predictor; $\theta=1\Rightarrow\bar f^{\,n}=2f^n-f^{n-1}$}
  \State $y_{c}^{n+1}\gets \Pi_{\{\|u\|_2\le\varepsilon_c\sqrt m\}}\!\big(y_{c}^{n}+\sigma_{c}\,R_{c}(g-X\bar f^{\,n})\big)$,\quad $c\in\{\mathrm{hi},\mathrm{lo}\}$
  \State $p_{d}^{n+1}\gets \Pi_{\{\|\cdot\|_\infty\le\alpha_d\}}\!\big(p_{d}^{n}+\sigma_{d}\,\partial_d\bar f^{\,n}\big)$,\quad $d\in\{x,y,z\}$
  \State $\tilde f^{\,n+1}\gets f^{n}-\tau\big(X^\top(R_{\mathrm{hi}}^\top y_{\mathrm{hi}}^{n+1}+R_{\mathrm{lo}}^\top y_{\mathrm{lo}}^{n+1})+\sum_d\partial_d^\top p_d^{n+1}\big)$
  \State $f^{n+1}\gets \max\!\big(0,\ \operatorname{shrink}(\tilde f^{\,n+1},\tau\beta)\big)$
\EndFor
\State \textbf{Output:} $f^{N}$.
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

The scalar Chambolle--Pock theorem~\citep{chambolle2011first} guarantees convergence of \eqref{eq:saddle} when a single step pair satisfies $\tau\sigma\|K\|^2<1$. Algorithm~\ref{alg:two} uses \emph{distinct} step sizes $\sigma_{\mathrm{hi}},\sigma_{\mathrm{lo}},\sigma_{x,y,z}$, a case this condition does not address. In practice the implementation also replaces the $\theta=1$ extrapolation by the He--Yuan over-relaxation $\bar f^{\,n}=f^n+\rho(f^n-f^{n-1})$ with $\rho\approx1.75$ as an accelerator, which converges for $\rho\in(0,2)$ under the same step-size condition~\citep{he2012convergence}; the stability condition we derive below is what is imposed on the step sizes in either case. The results of Section~\ref{sec:main} are stated for $\theta=1$. Section~\ref{sec:main} establishes convergence for distinct step sizes.

\section{Main Result}
\label{sec:main}

We analyze the preconditioned iteration in general form. Let $G,F^\ast$ be proper, convex, lower semicontinuous, $K$ linear, and let $T,\Sigma$ be symmetric positive definite. Write $\bar x^{\,n}=2x^n-x^{n-1}$ and run
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:iter}
\begin{cases}
y^{n+1}=(I+\Sigma\,\partial F^\ast)^{-1}\!\big(y^{n}+\Sigma K\bar x^{\,n}\big),\\[0.3em]
x^{n+1}=(I+T\,\partial G)^{-1}\!\big(x^{n}-T K^\top y^{n+1}\big),
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
which is the abstract form of Algorithm~\ref{alg:two}. For a symmetric positive definite $M$ write $\|u\|_M^2:=\langle u,Mu\rangle$, and define the weighted norms $\|\cdot\|_{T^{-1}}$, $\|\cdot\|_{\Sigma^{-1}}$ together with
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:Ltilde}
\widetilde L:=\big\|\Sigma^{1/2}K\,T^{1/2}\big\|.
\end{equation}
The restricted primal--dual gap on a set $B_1\times B_2$ is $\mathcal G_{B_1\times B_2}(x,y):=\sup_{y'\in B_2}\Phi(x,y')-\inf_{x'\in B_1}\Phi(x',y)$.

\begin{theorem}[Convergence with channel-dependent step sizes]
\label{thm:main}
Let $K$ be block-stacked with blocks $K_j$ as in \eqref{eq:K}, let $F^\ast=\sum_jF_j^\ast$ be block-separable, and let
\[
T=\tau I_n,
\qquad
\Sigma=\diag(\sigma_1I,\dots,\sigma_JI),
\qquad
\tau,\sigma_j>0 .
\]
Assume \eqref{eq:saddle} admits a saddle point $(\hat x,\hat y)$. Define the step-weighted normal operator
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:M}
M:=K^\top\Sigma K=\sum_{j}\sigma_j\,K_j^\top K_j
\end{equation}
and the weighted energy
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:energy}
\mathcal E_n
:=\tfrac12\|x^n-\hat x\|_{T^{-1}}^2+\tfrac12\|y^n-\hat y\|_{\Sigma^{-1}}^2
=\frac{\|x^n-\hat x\|^2}{2\tau}+\sum_j\frac{\|y_j^n-\hat y_j\|^2}{2\sigma_j}.
\end{equation}
If the step sizes satisfy
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:cond}
\tau\,\lambda_{\max}(M)<1,
\end{equation}
then the iteration \eqref{eq:iter} with $\theta=1$ satisfies the following.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\alph*)}]
\item \emph{(Energy monotonicity)} the weighted energy does not increase: for every $N\ge1$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:contraction}
\mathcal E_N\;\le\;\mathcal E_0 ;
\end{equation}
\item \emph{(Ergodic rate)} with $X_N=\frac1N\sum_{n=1}^Nx^n$ and $Y_N=\frac1N\sum_{n=1}^Ny^n$, for every bounded $B_1\times B_2$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:rate}
\mathcal G_{B_1\times B_2}(X_N,Y_N)\;\le\;\frac{\widetilde D}{N},
\qquad
\widetilde D:=\sup_{(x,y)\in B_1\times B_2}
\left[\frac{\|x^0-x\|^2}{2\tau}+\sum_j\frac{\|y_j^0-y_j\|^2}{2\sigma_j}\right];
\end{equation}
\item \emph{(Iterate convergence)} $(x^n,y^n)$ converges to a saddle point of \eqref{eq:saddle}.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Iteration \eqref{eq:iter} is the Chambolle--Pock method run in the metrics $\|\cdot\|_{T^{-1}}$ and $\|\cdot\|_{\Sigma^{-1}}$. Theorem~1 of \citet{chambolle2011first}, stated there for scalar steps and extended to symmetric positive definite $T$ and $\Sigma$ by \citet[\S3]{pock2011diagonal}, yields the energy bound \eqref{eq:contraction}, the $O(1/N)$ ergodic rate \eqref{eq:rate}, and the iterate convergence~(c) under the operator-norm condition $\widetilde L<1$, with the energy and the ergodic constant measured in those same weighted norms.

It remains to collapse $\widetilde L$ to a computable scalar. Because $T=\tau I$ is a multiple of the identity,
\[
\widetilde L^2
=\big\|\Sigma^{1/2}KT^{1/2}\big\|^2
=\lambda_{\max}\!\big(T^{1/2}K^\top\Sigma K\,T^{1/2}\big)
=\tau\,\lambda_{\max}\!\big(K^\top\Sigma K\big)
=\tau\,\lambda_{\max}(M),
\]
so $\widetilde L<1$ is exactly \eqref{eq:cond}. Since $\Sigma$ is block-diagonal and $K$ block-stacked, $K^\top\Sigma K=\sum_j\sigma_jK_j^\top K_j$, which is \eqref{eq:M}; expanding $\|\cdot\|_{T^{-1}}$ and $\|\cdot\|_{\Sigma^{-1}}$ blockwise gives the per-channel expressions in \eqref{eq:energy} and \eqref{eq:rate}.
\end{proof}

The step from $\widetilde L$ to $\lambda_{\max}(M)$ is what makes the condition usable: $\lambda_{\max}(M)$ is obtained from one power iteration on the action $u\mapsto Mu$, which requires only the existing forward and adjoint operators and never assembles $M$.

With $T$ and $\Sigma$ written out, every step size appears explicitly in the energy $\mathcal E_n$ and the ergodic constant $\widetilde D$: the primal error carries weight $1/(2\tau)$ and each dual block $j$ carries weight $1/(2\sigma_j)$. Part~(a) bounds the last iterate $f^N$, which is what Algorithm~\ref{alg:two} returns and what the experiments report; part~(b) bounds the ergodic average through the same per-block weights. Two consequences follow. First, raising $\sigma_{\mathrm{lo}}$ lowers the weight $1/(2\sigma_{\mathrm{lo}})$ on the low-frequency dual, shrinking that channel's share of the $O(1/N)$ ergodic constant $\widetilde D$, the quantitative content of a ``stronger near-DC correction.'' Second, $\sigma_{\mathrm{lo}}$ is not free: it also enters $M$ through the summand $\sigma_{\mathrm{lo}}X^\top R_{\mathrm{lo}}^\top R_{\mathrm{lo}}X$, so increasing it raises $\lambda_{\max}(M)$ and forces $\tau$ down to preserve \eqref{eq:cond}, inflating the primal weight $1/(2\tau)$. Since $\widetilde D$ weights every block error by the inverse of its step, the ergodic constant shrinks as the steps are taken as large as \eqref{eq:cond} allows; one therefore operates just within the stability boundary, at $\tau\lambda_{\max}(M)=1-\delta$. The optimal $\sigma_{\mathrm{lo}}$ balances the two effects; band separation (Remark~\ref{rmk:tight}) keeps the low channel's contribution to $\lambda_{\max}(M)$ small, so the down-weighting dominates over a wide range, as quantified in Remark~\ref{rmk:choose}.

\begin{remark}[Sharpness relative to the worst-channel surrogate]
\label{rmk:tight}
Condition \eqref{eq:cond} is sharper than applying the scalar rule to the stacked operator. The conservative substitution $\sigma_j\le\sigma_{\max}:=\max_j\sigma_j$ discards the channel structure and gives the worst-channel surrogate
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:worst-channel}
\tau\,\sigma_{\max}\|K\|^2<1,
\end{equation}
which treats every block as if it were multiplied by the largest dual step. Condition \eqref{eq:cond} instead retains the individual normal operators $\sigma_jK_j^\top K_j$ before taking the largest eigenvalue. The improvement therefore does not come from a different generic convergence theorem; it comes from exploiting the positive-semidefinite channel structure before reducing the stability condition to a scalar.

The size of the improvement is governed by band separation. If the dominant eigenvectors of the channel normal operators are close to mutually orthogonal, then $\lambda_{\max}(\sum_j\sigma_jA_j)$ is close to $\max_j\sigma_j\lambda_{\max}(A_j)$ rather than to $\sigma_{\max}\sum_j\lambda_{\max}(A_j)$, and the two bounds separate by roughly the number of channels. For the two data channels this separation is produced by the shared square-root ramp: $A_{\mathrm{lo}}$ is supported near DC, where the ramp suppresses the singular values of $X$, whereas $A_{\mathrm{hi}}$ attains its leading eigenvalue in the mid-band, so the dominant subspaces are nearly disjoint and $\lambda_{\max}(M)$ falls well below \eqref{eq:worst-channel}. Condition \eqref{eq:cond} therefore licenses strictly larger $\tau$ and $\sigma_{\mathrm{lo}}$ than the worst-channel bound. This larger admissible step is the mechanism behind faster low-frequency convergence. Table~\ref{tab:stability} reports the separation for the operator used in the experiments. Without the shared ramp the two bands overlap, the separation is lost, and the certified amplification provides no benefit.
\end{remark}

The two-channel case admits an exact characterization of the admissible low-band step, obtained by isolating $\sigma_{\mathrm{lo}}$ in \eqref{eq:cond}.

\begin{theorem}[Exact admissible low-band step]
\label{thm:two-channel}
Let
\[
K_{\rm hi}=R_{\rm hi}X,
\qquad
K_{\rm lo}=R_{\rm lo}X,
\]
and define the two data-channel normal operators
\[
A_{\rm hi}:=K_{\rm hi}^{\top}K_{\rm hi}
=
X^\top R_{\rm hi}^{\top}R_{\rm hi}X,
\qquad
A_{\rm lo}:=K_{\rm lo}^{\top}K_{\rm lo}
=
X^\top R_{\rm lo}^{\top}R_{\rm lo}X .
\]
Let
\[
B:=
\sum_{d\in\{x,y,z\}}
\sigma_d\partial_d^\top\partial_d,
\qquad
M(\sigma_{\rm hi},\sigma_{\rm lo})
:=
B+\sigma_{\rm hi}A_{\rm hi}
+\sigma_{\rm lo}A_{\rm lo},
\]
so that $M(\sigma_{\rm hi},\sigma_{\rm lo})$ is the operator \eqref{eq:M} for Algorithm~\ref{alg:two}, and the stability condition \eqref{eq:cond} reads
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:cond-two-channel}
\tau\lambda_{\max}
\!\left(
B+\sigma_{\mathrm{hi}}A_{\mathrm{hi}}
+\sigma_{\mathrm{lo}}A_{\mathrm{lo}}
\right)<1.
\end{equation}
Fix $\tau$, $\sigma_{\rm hi}$, and the TV steps $\{\sigma_d\}$, and define the stability budget remaining after the high-frequency and regularization blocks have been accounted for,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:Qhi}
Q_{\rm hi}
:=
\tau^{-1}I
-
B
-
\sigma_{\rm hi}A_{\rm hi}.
\end{equation}
If $Q_{\rm hi}\succ0$, then \eqref{eq:cond-two-channel} is equivalent to
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:sigma-lo-exact}
\sigma_{\rm lo}\,
\lambda_{\max}
\!\left(
Q_{\rm hi}^{-1/2}
A_{\rm lo}
Q_{\rm hi}^{-1/2}
\right)
<1,
\end{equation}
and the supremal admissible low-channel step is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:sigma-lo-max}
\sigma_{\rm lo}^{\max}
=
\frac{1}{
\lambda_{\max}
\!\left(
Q_{\rm hi}^{-1/2}
A_{\rm lo}
Q_{\rm hi}^{-1/2}
\right)
}
=
\inf_{\substack{u\neq0\\u^\top A_{\mathrm{lo}}u>0}}
\frac{
\tau^{-1}\|u\|^2
-
u^\top Bu
-
\sigma_{\mathrm{hi}}u^\top A_{\mathrm{hi}}u
}{
u^\top A_{\mathrm{lo}}u
}.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For Algorithm~\ref{alg:two} the blocks of $K$ are $K_{\rm hi}$, $K_{\rm lo}$ and $\{\partial_d\}$, so \eqref{eq:M} gives
$M=\sigma_{\rm hi}A_{\rm hi}+\sigma_{\rm lo}A_{\rm lo}+\sum_d\sigma_d\partial_d^\top\partial_d
=B+\sigma_{\rm hi}A_{\rm hi}+\sigma_{\rm lo}A_{\rm lo}$,
and \eqref{eq:cond} becomes \eqref{eq:cond-two-channel}. Since $M$ is symmetric,
\[
\tau\lambda_{\max}(M)<1
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
M\prec\tau^{-1}I
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\sigma_{\rm lo}A_{\rm lo}
\prec
Q_{\rm hi},
\]
by the definition \eqref{eq:Qhi} of $Q_{\rm hi}$. Assume $Q_{\rm hi}\succ0$. Congruence by $Q_{\rm hi}^{-1/2}$ preserves the Loewner ordering, so this is equivalent to
\[
\sigma_{\rm lo}\,
Q_{\rm hi}^{-1/2}
A_{\rm lo}
Q_{\rm hi}^{-1/2}
\prec I .
\]
The matrix $Q_{\rm hi}^{-1/2}A_{\rm lo}Q_{\rm hi}^{-1/2}$ is symmetric positive semidefinite, so the preceding matrix inequality holds if and only if its largest eigenvalue is less than one, which is \eqref{eq:sigma-lo-exact}. Solving for $\sigma_{\rm lo}$ gives the first expression in \eqref{eq:sigma-lo-max}, and the second follows from the generalized Rayleigh-quotient characterization of $\lambda_{\max}(Q_{\rm hi}^{-1/2}A_{\rm lo}Q_{\rm hi}^{-1/2})$.
\end{proof}

Equation~\eqref{eq:sigma-lo-max} gives a mode-by-mode reading of the two-channel stability limit: the low-frequency step is limited by the image-space direction in which the ratio of remaining stability budget to low-channel energy is smallest.

\begin{remark}[Choosing the step sizes]
\label{rmk:choose}
Condition \eqref{eq:cond} constrains only the product of the primal step and the overall dual scale, not the relative sizes of the dual steps. Writing
\[
\sigma_{\mathrm{hi}}=s,
\qquad
\sigma_{\mathrm{lo}}=r_{\mathrm{lo}}\,s,
\qquad
\sigma_d=r_d\,s,
\qquad
\widehat M
:=
A_{\mathrm{hi}}+r_{\mathrm{lo}}A_{\mathrm{lo}}+\sum_dr_d\partial_d^\top\partial_d,
\qquad
\ell:=\lambda_{\max}(\widehat M),
\]
separates the two, since $M=s\widehat M$ and \eqref{eq:cond} reads $\tau s\,\ell<1$. With safety margin $\delta\in(0,1)$ one saturates it at $\tau s=(1-\delta)/\ell$, leaving the ratios $r_{\mathrm{lo}},r_d$ and the primal--dual balance $\tau/s$ free; $\ell$ is again a single power iteration.

Increasing $r_{\mathrm{lo}}$ has the two opposing effects identified after Theorem~\ref{thm:main}: it lowers the weight $1/(2\sigma_{\mathrm{lo}})$ carried by the low-frequency dual, and it raises $\ell$, reducing the admissible $\tau s$. Band separation (Remark~\ref{rmk:tight}) makes $\ell$ grow slowly in $r_{\mathrm{lo}}$; moderately large ratios $r_{\mathrm{lo}}\approx4$ therefore remain near-optimal. The useful range ends at the crossover where $A_{\mathrm{lo}}$ begins to determine $\lambda_{\max}(\widehat M)$: past that point every further increase in $r_{\mathrm{lo}}$ is paid for by a proportional reduction in $\tau$, with no net gain in low-frequency correction. Table~\ref{tab:stability} locates this crossover for the operator used in the experiments, and Theorem~\ref{thm:two-channel} gives the exact ceiling.
\end{remark}

\section{Dyadic Multi-Channel Fidelity}
\label{sec:dyadic}

The two-channel split is the depth-$2$ instance of a more general construction: rather than a single low/high partition, the ramp-weighted residual can be decomposed into a \emph{dyadic} bank of octave-localized channels, each with its own dual step. This refines the per-channel correction across the whole low-frequency tail, not just a single near-DC band, at only a small reduction of the primal step. The analysis of Section~\ref{sec:main} applies verbatim because it was stated for a block-stacked $K$ and a block-separable $F^\ast$; only the operator $M$ and its spectrum change.

\subsection{The dyadic filter bank}
\label{sec:dyadic-setup}

Let $\omega$ index the detector frequency along the channel direction and let $\Omega:=|b_{00}|$ denote the largest detector-frequency magnitude (half the detector extent), so that the single-channel fidelity weight is the square-root ramp $R_{\mathrm{full}}=\sqrt{|\omega|}$ applied per view by an FFT along the detector. For a target depth $k$, define square-root Hanning windows of geometrically shrinking half-width,
\begin{equation}
    H_{i}(\omega)=\tfrac12\bigl(1+\cos(\pi\,\omega\,2^{i}/\Omega)\bigr)\,\ind_{\{|\omega|\le \Omega/2^{i}\}},\qquad i=0,1,\dots,k-1,
    \label{eq:dyadic-hann}
\end{equation}
so $H_{0}$ covers the full band, $H_{1}$ the lower half, and $H_{i}$ the near-DC core of half-width $\Omega/2^{i}$. The band-pass windows are the telescoping differences
\begin{equation}
    P_{0}=1-H_{1},\qquad P_{i}=H_{i}-H_{i+1}\ (0<i<k-1),\qquad P_{k-1}=H_{k-1},
    \label{eq:dyadic-bands}
\end{equation}
which form a partition of unity: $\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}P_{i}(\omega)\equiv1$. Channel $0$ is the top octave $|\omega|\in[\Omega/2,\Omega]$, channel $i$ the dyadic shell $[\Omega/2^{i+1},\Omega/2^{i}]$, and channel $k-1$ the closed low-frequency tail $[0,\Omega/2^{k-1}]$. Each band filter is the ramp-weighted square root of its window, acting as a detector-domain multiplier,
\begin{equation}
    R_{i}:=\sqrt{|\omega|\,P_{i}(\omega)},\qquad\text{so that}\qquad
    \sum_{i=0}^{k-1}R_{i}^\top R_{i}=|\omega|\sum_{i}P_{i}=|\omega|=R_{\mathrm{full}}^\top R_{\mathrm{full}}.
    \label{eq:dyadic-partition}
\end{equation}
The split is therefore \emph{lossless}: the stacked data term reproduces the single-channel ramp weighting exactly (to machine precision), so it changes only the iteration's preconditioning, not the data-fidelity content. The $k$-channel constrained problem replaces the two data constraints of \eqref{eq:two} by
\begin{equation}
    \|R_{i}(Xf-g)\|_{2}\le\varepsilon_{i}\sqrt{m},\qquad i=0,\dots,k-1,
    \label{eq:dyadic-constraints}
\end{equation}
and the stacked operator and dual preconditioner become
\begin{equation}
    K=\begin{bmatrix}R_{0}X\\ \vdots\\ R_{k-1}X\\ \partial_x\\ \partial_y\\ \partial_z\end{bmatrix},\qquad
    \Sigma=\diag\!\bigl(\sigma_{0}I,\dots,\sigma_{k-1}I,\ \sigma_x I,\ \sigma_y I,\ \sigma_z I\bigr).
    \label{eq:dyadic-K}
\end{equation}
Algorithm~\ref{alg:dyadic} states the resulting iteration (with the He--Yuan over-relaxation used in the implementation); it is Algorithm~\ref{alg:two} with the two data blocks replaced by $k$ band blocks, each projected onto its own $\ell_2$ ball at its own rate $\sigma_i$.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Dyadic $k$-channel PDHG (He--Yuan relaxed)}
\label{alg:dyadic}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\State \textbf{Input:} $g,X,\{R_i\}_{i=0}^{k-1}$, regularization $\alpha_{x,y,z},\beta$, tolerances $\{\varepsilon_i\}$, steps $\tau,\{\sigma_i\},\sigma_{x,y,z}$ with $\tau\,\lambda_{\max}(M)<1$ (eq.~\eqref{eq:dyadic-M}), relaxation $\rho\in(1,2)$.
\State \textbf{Initialize:} $f^{0}=0$; all duals $=0$; $\bar f^{0}=f^{0}$.
\For{$n=0,1,2,\dots$}
  \State $w\gets X^\top\!\sum_{i}R_{i}^\top y_{i}^{n}+\sum_d\partial_d^\top p_d^{n}$
  \State $\tilde f\gets \max\!\bigl(0,\ \operatorname{shrink}(f^{n}-\tau w,\ \tau\beta)\bigr)$;\quad $\bar f\gets 2\tilde f-f^{n}$ \Comment{predictor ($\theta=1$)}
  \State $y_{i}^{+}\gets \Pi_{\{\|u\|_2\le\varepsilon_i\sqrt m\}}\!\bigl(y_{i}^{n}+\sigma_{i}R_{i}(X\bar f-g)\bigr),\quad i=0,\dots,k-1$
  \State $p_{d}^{+}\gets \Pi_{\{\|\cdot\|_\infty\le\alpha_d\}}\!\bigl(p_{d}^{n}+\sigma_{d}\,\partial_d\bar f\bigr),\ d\in\{x,y,z\}$
  \State $v^{n+1}\gets v^{n}-\rho\,(v^{n}-v^{+})$ for every variable $v\in\{f,\,y_i,\,p_d\}$ \Comment{He--Yuan over-relaxation}
\EndFor
\State \textbf{Output:} $f^{N}$.
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Convergence and the dyadic stability bound}

For the dyadic bank, define the channel normal operators
\[
A_i
:=
X^\top R_i^\top R_iX,
\qquad
i=0,\ldots,k-1,
\]
and collect the fixed regularization contribution into
\[
B
:=
\sum_{d\in\{x,y,z\}}
\sigma_d\partial_d^\top\partial_d.
\]
The step-weighted normal operator governing the PDHG stability
condition is therefore
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:dyadic-M}
M_k
=
B+M_{\mathrm{data},k},
\qquad
M_{\mathrm{data},k}
:=
\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\sigma_iA_i.
\end{equation}
For the $\theta=1$ iteration, the convergence argument of
Theorem~\ref{thm:main} applies whenever
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:dyadic-cond}
\tau\lambda_{\max}(M_k)<1.
\end{equation}
As in the two-channel case, this condition retains the individual
positive-semidefinite channel operators before taking the largest
eigenvalue.  It is therefore sharper than replacing all dual steps
by $\sigma_{\max}:=\max_i\sigma_i$, which would give the
worst-channel surrogate
\[
\tau\sigma_{\max}
\lambda_{\max}
\left(
\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}A_i
+
\sum_d\partial_d^\top\partial_d
\right)<1.
\]

The dyadic structure permits a stronger conclusion.  Rather than
bounding $\lambda_{\max}(M_{\mathrm{data},k})$ by the sum of the
individual channel norms, we evaluate the weighted sum of the
dyadic windows before maximizing over frequency.  This preserves
the spectral complementarity built into the filter bank.

\begin{proposition}[Exact stability budget for adding one dyadic channel]
\label{prop:dyadic-budget}
Suppose channels $0,\ldots,q-1$ have already been assigned steps
$\sigma_0,\ldots,\sigma_{q-1}$, and define the remaining stability
budget
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:Qq}
Q_q
:=
\tau^{-1}I
-
B
-
\sum_{i=0}^{q-1}\sigma_iA_i.
\end{equation}
If $Q_q\succ0$, then adding channel $q$ preserves the stability
condition \eqref{eq:dyadic-cond} if and only if
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:dyadic-generalized-eig}
\sigma_q
\lambda_{\max}
\left(
Q_q^{-1/2}A_qQ_q^{-1/2}
\right)
<1.
\end{equation}
Consequently, the supremal admissible step for channel $q$ is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:dyadic-sigma-max}
\sigma_q^{\max}
=
\frac{1}{
\lambda_{\max}
\left(
Q_q^{-1/2}A_qQ_q^{-1/2}
\right)
}.
\end{equation}
Equivalently,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:dyadic-rayleigh}
\sigma_q^{\max}
=
\inf_{\substack{u\neq0\\u^\top A_qu>0}}
\frac{u^\top Q_qu}{u^\top A_qu}.
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
After channels $0,\ldots,q-1$ have been included, adding channel
$q$ gives
\[
B+\sum_{i=0}^{q-1}\sigma_iA_i+\sigma_qA_q
\prec
\tau^{-1}I.
\]
By the definition of $Q_q$, this is equivalent to
\[
\sigma_qA_q\prec Q_q.
\]
Since $Q_q\succ0$, congruence by $Q_q^{-1/2}$ preserves the
Loewner ordering and gives
\[
\sigma_q
Q_q^{-1/2}A_qQ_q^{-1/2}
\prec I.
\]
The matrix $Q_q^{-1/2}A_qQ_q^{-1/2}$ is symmetric positive
semidefinite, so the preceding inequality holds if and only if
\[
\sigma_q
\lambda_{\max}
\left(
Q_q^{-1/2}A_qQ_q^{-1/2}
\right)<1.
\]
This proves \eqref{eq:dyadic-generalized-eig} and
\eqref{eq:dyadic-sigma-max}.  The Rayleigh-quotient form
\eqref{eq:dyadic-rayleigh} follows from the standard generalized
eigenvalue characterization.
\end{proof}

\begin{assumption}[Fourier-slice diagonalization]
\label{assu:fourier_slice}
There exist orthonormal systems
$\{u_{\theta,\omega}\}$ spanning $\operatorname{range}(X^\top)\subseteq\R^n$ and
$\{e_{\theta,\omega}\}$ spanning $\operatorname{range}(X)\subseteq\R^m$, indexed by sampled angle
$\theta$ and detector frequency $\omega$, such that
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:fourier_slice}
Xu_{\theta,\omega}
=
s_X(\theta,\omega)e_{\theta,\omega},
\qquad
R_ie_{\theta,\omega}
=
W_i(\omega)e_{\theta,\omega}.
\end{equation}
For the dyadic filters,
\[
|W_i(\omega)|^2
=
|\omega|P_i(\omega).
\]
We further write
\[
s_*^2
:=
\max_{\theta,\omega}
s_X(\theta,\omega)^2,
\]
which is finite for the fixed discrete imaging system. On $\ker X$ every channel normal operator $A_i=X^\top R_i^\top R_iX$ vanishes, so the eigenvalue computations below are unaffected by the complement of $\operatorname{range}(X^\top)$; in particular the system $\{u_{\theta,\omega}\}$ need not span $\R^n$, and indeed cannot when the number of sampled $(\theta,\omega)$ pairs is smaller than $n$, as in the limited-angle setting of Section~\ref{sec:experiments}.

Assumption~\ref{assu:fourier_slice} is an idealization. It holds exactly for a parallel-beam operator sampled over the full angular range, where the Fourier slice theorem makes the per-view detector Fourier modes singular vectors of $X$. For the fan-beam and cone-beam operators of interest it is an approximation, in which the divergent geometry and the limited arc perturb the singular vectors away from the per-view Fourier basis. It is used only in Proposition~\ref{prop:dyadic-depth}, to sum the dyadic windows before maximizing over frequency; the stability condition \eqref{eq:dyadic-cond} and the exact budget of Proposition~\ref{prop:dyadic-budget} do not rely on it.
\end{assumption}

\begin{proposition}[Critical geometric schedule for the dyadic bank]
\label{prop:dyadic-depth}
Let
\[
\sigma_i=\sigma_0\rho_\sigma^i,
\qquad
\rho_\sigma\ge1,
\]
and let
\[
M_{\mathrm{data},k}
=
\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}
\sigma_iX^\top R_i^\top R_iX.
\]
Under Assumption~\ref{assu:fourier_slice},
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:dyadic-spectrum-exact}
\lambda_{\max}(M_{\mathrm{data},k})
=
\sigma_0
\max_{\theta,\omega}
\left\{
s_X(\theta,\omega)^2
|\omega|
\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}
\rho_\sigma^iP_i(\omega)
\right\}.
\end{equation}

For the nested Hanning partition \eqref{eq:dyadic-hann}--%
\eqref{eq:dyadic-partition}, the weighted window sum satisfies
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:weighted-window-bound}
|\omega|
\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\rho_\sigma^iP_i(\omega)
\le
\begin{cases}
\Omega,
& 1\le\rho_\sigma\le2,
\\[0.4em]
\Omega
\left(\dfrac{\rho_\sigma}{2}\right)^{k-1},
& \rho_\sigma>2.
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
Consequently,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:dyadic-depth-bound}
\lambda_{\max}(M_{\mathrm{data},k})
\le
\begin{cases}
\sigma_0\Omega s_*^2,
& 1\le\rho_\sigma\le2,
\\[0.4em]
\sigma_0\Omega s_*^2
\left(\dfrac{\rho_\sigma}{2}\right)^{k-1},
& \rho_\sigma>2.
\end{cases}
\end{equation}

In particular, for the matched schedule
\[
\boxed{\rho_\sigma=2}
\]
the data-channel stability cost is bounded independently of the
number of dyadic bands:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:dyadic-k-independent}
\lambda_{\max}(M_{\mathrm{data},k})
\le
\sigma_0\Omega s_*^2
\qquad
\text{for every }k.
\end{equation}
Thus, with the regularization steps fixed,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:dyadic-total-bound}
\lambda_{\max}(M_k)
\le
\lambda_{\max}(B)
+
\sigma_0\Omega s_*^2,
\end{equation}
so the certified primal step need not decrease with the filter-bank
depth $k$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Under Assumption~\ref{assu:fourier_slice},
\[
A_i
=
X^\top R_i^\top R_iX
\]
is diagonal in the basis $\{u_{\theta,\omega}\}$, with eigenvalue
\[
s_X(\theta,\omega)^2
|W_i(\omega)|^2
=
s_X(\theta,\omega)^2
|\omega|P_i(\omega).
\]
Therefore all of the data-channel normal operators are
simultaneously diagonal in this basis, and
\[
M_{\mathrm{data},k}
=
\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\sigma_iA_i
\]
has eigenvalue
\[
\sigma_0
s_X(\theta,\omega)^2
|\omega|
\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}
\rho_\sigma^iP_i(\omega)
\]
on $u_{\theta,\omega}$.  Taking the largest such eigenvalue gives
\eqref{eq:dyadic-spectrum-exact}.

It remains to bound the weighted dyadic partition
\[
S_k(\omega)
:=
\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}
\rho_\sigma^iP_i(\omega).
\]
Recall that
\[
P_0=1-H_1,
\qquad
P_i=H_i-H_{i+1},
\quad
1\le i<k-1,
\qquad
P_{k-1}=H_{k-1}.
\]
Substitution gives
\begin{align}
S_k(\omega)
&=
1-H_1
+
\sum_{i=1}^{k-2}
\rho_\sigma^i(H_i-H_{i+1})
+
\rho_\sigma^{k-1}H_{k-1}
\nonumber\\
&=
1
+
(\rho_\sigma-1)
\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}
\rho_\sigma^{j-1}H_j(\omega).
\label{eq:dyadic-telescope}
\end{align}
This identity is the point at which the nested dyadic structure
gives a sharper result than a Weyl bound on the individual channel
operators.

Set
\[
r:=\frac{|\omega|}{\Omega}\in[0,1].
\]
For $\omega\neq0$, let
\[
J(\omega)
:=
\min
\left\{
k-1,
\left\lfloor
\log_2\frac{1}{r}
\right\rfloor
\right\}.
\]
Because $H_j$ is supported on
\[
|\omega|\le\frac{\Omega}{2^j},
\]
we have $H_j(\omega)=0$ for $j>J(\omega)$, while
$0\le H_j(\omega)\le1$.  Hence \eqref{eq:dyadic-telescope} gives
\begin{align}
S_k(\omega)
&\le
1+
(\rho_\sigma-1)
\sum_{j=1}^{J(\omega)}
\rho_\sigma^{j-1}
\nonumber\\
&=
\rho_\sigma^{J(\omega)}.
\label{eq:S-bound}
\end{align}
By the definition of $J(\omega)$ we have
$J(\omega)\le\log_2(1/r)$ in both branches of the minimum, hence
\[
r\le 2^{-J(\omega)} .
\]
Therefore
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:rS-bound}
rS_k(\omega)
\le
\left(\frac{\rho_\sigma}{2}\right)^{J(\omega)}.
\end{equation}

If $1\le\rho_\sigma\le2$, the right-hand side of
\eqref{eq:rS-bound} is at most one.  Hence
\[
|\omega|S_k(\omega)
=
\Omega rS_k(\omega)
\le\Omega,
\]
independently of $k$.

If $\rho_\sigma>2$, then
$J(\omega)\le k-1$, so
\[
rS_k(\omega)
\le
\left(\frac{\rho_\sigma}{2}\right)^{k-1},
\]
which yields the second line of
\eqref{eq:weighted-window-bound}.

At $\omega=0$, every data-channel multiplier contains the ramp
factor $|\omega|$, so the left-hand side of
\eqref{eq:weighted-window-bound} is zero and the same bound holds.

Combining \eqref{eq:dyadic-spectrum-exact} with
\eqref{eq:weighted-window-bound} and
$s_X(\theta,\omega)^2\le s_*^2$ proves
\eqref{eq:dyadic-depth-bound}.  Finally, since
\[
M_k=B+M_{\mathrm{data},k}
\]
with both terms positive semidefinite,
\[
\lambda_{\max}(M_k)
\le
\lambda_{\max}(B)
+
\lambda_{\max}(M_{\mathrm{data},k}),
\]
which gives \eqref{eq:dyadic-total-bound}.
\end{proof}



\begin{remark}[Why the dyadic bound is sharper than Weyl]
\label{rmk:dyadic-tight}
A channel-by-channel norm bound gives
\[
\lambda_{\max}(M_{\mathrm{data},k})
\le
\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}
\sigma_i\|R_iX\|_2^2.
\]
If one additionally assumes
$\|R_iX\|_2^2\approx2^{-i}\|R_0X\|_2^2$, then the choice
$\sigma_i=2^i\sigma_0$ makes every summand approximately equal and
produces an $O(k)$ upper bound.  This is the Weyl estimate used in
a channelwise analysis.

Proposition~\ref{prop:dyadic-depth} is sharper because it does not
maximize each dyadic band separately before summing them.  Under
Assumption~\ref{assu:fourier_slice}, the channel normal operators
are simultaneously diagonalized, so the correct quantity is
instead
\[
\max_{\theta,\omega}
s_X(\theta,\omega)^2|\omega|
\sum_i\sigma_iP_i(\omega).
\]
The dyadic windows can therefore be summed first.  Their nested
telescoping structure yields
\[
|\omega|
\sum_i2^iP_i(\omega)
\le\Omega,
\]
which removes the factor $k$ entirely.

Thus three levels of stability estimate can be distinguished:
\[
\underbrace{
\tau\sigma_{\max}\|K\|^2<1
}_{\text{worst-channel scalar surrogate}},
\]
\[
\underbrace{
\lambda_{\max}(M_{\mathrm{data},k})
\le
\sum_i\sigma_i\|R_iX\|_2^2
}_{\text{channelwise Weyl bound}},
\]
and
\[
\underbrace{
\lambda_{\max}(M_{\mathrm{data},k})
=
\max_{\theta,\omega}
s_X(\theta,\omega)^2|\omega|
\sum_i\sigma_iP_i(\omega)
}_{\text{dyadic spectral bound}}.
\]
The final expression retains both the channel-specific steps and the
frequency partition before taking the spectral maximum, and is the
source of the depth-independent bound.

The value $\rho_\sigma=2$ is therefore critical in a precise sense:
it is the largest geometric amplification factor for which
\eqref{eq:dyadic-depth-bound} remains independent of $k$.  Choosing
$\rho_\sigma<2$ is more conservative and gives less amplification
toward DC, whereas $\rho_\sigma>2$ produces a certified stability
cost growing at most as
$(\rho_\sigma/2)^{k-1}$.
\end{remark}
\subsection{Hyperparameter selection for the dyadic bank}
\label{sec:dyadic-hyper}

The dyadic formulation introduces four groups of design parameters:
the number of bands $k$, the relative dual-step schedule
$\{\sigma_i/\sigma_0\}$, the per-band discrepancy tolerances
$\{\varepsilon_i\}$, and the overall primal--dual step scale.
These parameters do not play equivalent roles.  The step sizes
$\tau$ and $\{\sigma_i\}$ affect only the optimization trajectory
provided that the underlying constrained problem is fixed, whereas
the tolerances $\{\varepsilon_i\}$ determine the feasible set.
The depth $k$ has both numerical and modeling consequences: it
determines how finely the residual is partitioned and, because the
resulting constraints are imposed separately, also determines the
number of data-fidelity constraints.

This distinction is useful when tuning the method.  We therefore
select the parameters in the order
\[
k
\quad\longrightarrow\quad
\rho_\sigma
\quad\longrightarrow\quad
\{\varepsilon_i\}
\quad\longrightarrow\quad
(\sigma_0,\tau),
\]
so that the spectral partition and the reconstruction model are fixed
before the globally stable step scale is chosen.

\paragraph{Number of bands $k$.}
The useful depth of the bank is limited by detector-frequency
resolution.  The support of the $i$th nested Hanning window decreases
geometrically, with characteristic half-width
\[
\frac{\Omega}{2^i}.
\]
Consequently, each additional octave contains approximately half as
many discrete detector-frequency samples as the preceding octave.
Once the innermost band contains only a few Fourier samples, further
subdivision no longer represents a meaningful smooth frequency
partition: the nominal Hanning taper is represented by too few grid
points and the distinction between adjacent low-frequency channels
becomes primarily a discretization effect.

Let $m_0$ denote the minimum desired number of detector-frequency
samples in the innermost octave.  A convenient depth rule is therefore
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:dyadic-k}
k
=
\left\lfloor
\log_2\left(\frac{N_{\rm det}}{m_0}\right)
\right\rfloor,
\qquad
k\ge2.
\end{equation}
For example, $N_{\rm det}=1024$ and $m_0=8$ give $k=7$.

Equation~\eqref{eq:dyadic-k} should be interpreted as a numerical
resolution criterion rather than a reconstruction-resolution
criterion.  The bank is constructed in detector-frequency space, so
its maximum useful depth is governed primarily by $N_{\rm det}$ and
the filter discretization, not directly by the number of reconstructed
pixels or voxels.  Changing the reconstruction grid can nevertheless
change the spectrum of $X$ and hence the benefit obtained from the
additional low-frequency channels.

More generally, for an arbitrary detector size one may define
\[
n_i
:=
\#\{\omega_\ell:P_i(\omega_\ell)>0\}
\]
on the discrete FFT grid and choose the largest $k$ for which
$n_{k-1}\ge m_0$.  This avoids ambiguity from rounding when
$N_{\rm det}$ is not a power of two.

\paragraph{Relative dual-step schedule $\rho_\sigma$.}
We parameterize the data-channel steps geometrically,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:dyadic-step-schedule}
\sigma_i=\sigma_0\rho_\sigma^i,
\qquad i=0,\ldots,k-1.
\end{equation}
The purpose of increasing $\sigma_i$ toward DC is to compensate for
the progressively weaker ramp-weighted response of the low-frequency
channels.

The dyadic stability result of
Proposition~\ref{prop:dyadic-depth} gives a more precise criterion for
choosing $\rho_\sigma$.  Under the Fourier-slice approximation,
\[
\lambda_{\max}(M_{\rm data})
=
\sigma_0
\max_{\theta,\omega}
s_X(\theta,\omega)^2|\omega|
\sum_i\rho_\sigma^iP_i(\omega).
\]
The nested dyadic construction implies
\[
|\omega|
\sum_i\rho_\sigma^iP_i(\omega)
\le \Omega
\qquad\text{for}\qquad
1\le\rho_\sigma\le2,
\]
independently of the depth $k$.  Thus any schedule with
$1\le\rho_\sigma\le2$ has a depth-independent certified data-channel
stability cost.

The endpoint
\[
\boxed{\rho_\sigma=2}
\]
is distinguished because it is the largest geometric amplification
base with this property.  It applies the strongest octave-to-octave
increase permitted by the depth-independent bound.  In contrast, for
$\rho_\sigma>2$ the stability bound grows as
\[
\left(\frac{\rho_\sigma}{2}\right)^{k-1},
\]
so sufficiently deep low-frequency channels eventually dominate the
stability constraint.

Accordingly, $\rho_\sigma=2$ is a natural stability-critical default.
Values $1<\rho_\sigma<2$ provide more conservative low-frequency
amplification, while $\rho_\sigma>2$ should be used only when an
explicit computation of $\lambda_{\max}(M)$ demonstrates that the
additional amplification is worth the corresponding loss of primal
step size.

We emphasize that ``critical'' here refers to the stability scaling,
not to finite-iteration optimality.  The value of $\rho_\sigma$ that
minimizes a particular reconstruction error after a fixed iteration
budget may differ from $2$ and is problem dependent.

\paragraph{Per-band tolerances $\varepsilon_i$.}
Unlike the step sizes, the discrepancy tolerances alter the
optimization problem itself.  The dyadic identity
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:dyadic-energy-identity}
\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}
R_i^\top R_i
=
R_{\rm full}^\top R_{\rm full}
\end{equation}
implies, for every residual $r$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:dyadic-residual-energy}
\|R_{\rm full}r\|_2^2
=
\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\|R_ir\|_2^2.
\end{equation}
This identity means that the filter bank is lossless at the level of
quadratic residual energy.  It does \emph{not}, however, make the
collection of separate constraints
\[
\|R_ir\|_2\le\varepsilon_i\sqrt m,
\qquad i=0,\ldots,k-1,
\]
equivalent to the original single constraint
\[
\|R_{\rm full}r\|_2\le\varepsilon\sqrt m.
\]
Indeed, the separate constraints imply
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:tolerance-total}
\|R_{\rm full}r\|_2^2
\le
m\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\varepsilon_i^2.
\end{equation}
Thus the choice of $\{\varepsilon_i\}$ determines how the total
discrepancy budget is distributed across frequency.

A simple total-budget-preserving choice is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:tolerance-budget}
\varepsilon_i
=
\varepsilon\sqrt{w_i},
\qquad
w_i\ge0,
\qquad
\sum_iw_i=1.
\end{equation}
Then \eqref{eq:tolerance-total} gives
\[
\|R_{\rm full}r\|_2
\le
\varepsilon\sqrt m
\]
whenever all band constraints are satisfied.
The uniform allocation $w_i=1/k$ corresponds to
\[
\varepsilon_i=\frac{\varepsilon}{\sqrt{k}}.
\]
This preserves the original total discrepancy radius, although the
feasible set is still not identical to the single-ball feasible set
because the discrepancy is constrained separately in each band.

A less conservative allocation accounts for the effective size of
each band.  Let $m_i$ denote the number, or an effective number, of
detector-frequency degrees of freedom represented by channel $i$.
Setting
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:tolerance-dof}
w_i=\frac{m_i}{\sum_jm_j}
\end{equation}
assigns a discrepancy radius proportional to the square root of the
band dimension.  This prevents a narrow near-DC channel from receiving
the same absolute residual budget as a much wider high-frequency
channel merely because both are embedded in an $m$-dimensional
sinogram vector.

When a noise covariance $C_g$ for the sinogram residual is available,
a more directly statistical choice is to match each tolerance to the
expected filtered noise energy.  For a zero-mean residual perturbation
$\eta$ with covariance $C_g$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:filtered-noise-energy}
\mathbb E\|R_i\eta\|_2^2
=
\operatorname{tr}(R_iC_gR_i^\top).
\end{equation}
One may therefore choose radii of the form
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:tolerance-noise}
\varepsilon_i\sqrt m
=
c_\varepsilon
\sqrt{\operatorname{tr}(R_iC_gR_i^\top)},
\end{equation}
where $c_\varepsilon$ controls the desired discrepancy confidence
margin.  For white residual noise, $C_g=\nu^2I$, this reduces to
scaling the tolerance according to
$\|R_i\|_F$.

For DBT, the statistical allocation can be deliberately modified near
DC to account for model mismatch such as scatter, blank-scan error, or
beam-hardening effects, which are known to produce low-spatial-
frequency inconsistencies in the underlying reconstruction model.
The use of filtered discrepancy specifically to de-emphasize such
low-frequency inconsistencies is part of the motivation of the
single-channel DBT formulation of \citet{sidky2025accurate}.
Accordingly, one may introduce a low-frequency inflation factor
$a_i\ge1$ and use
\[
\varepsilon_i
=
a_i\varepsilon_i^{\rm noise},
\]
with $a_i$ increasing only for the lowest octaves.

\paragraph{Global dual scale and primal step.}
After $k$, $\rho_\sigma$, and the relative regularization steps have
been fixed, it is convenient to separate the \emph{relative} dual
preconditioner from its global scale.  Write
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:dual-common-scale}
\sigma_i=s\,r_i,
\qquad
r_i=\rho_\sigma^i,
\qquad
\sigma_d=s\,r_d,
\end{equation}
where $s>0$ is a common dual scale.  Then
\[
M=s\widehat M,
\]
with
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:M-ratio}
\widehat M
=
\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}
r_iX^\top R_i^\top R_iX
+
\sum_d
r_d\partial_d^\top\partial_d.
\end{equation}
Let
\[
\ell:=\lambda_{\max}(\widehat M),
\]
estimated by power iteration.  The exact stability condition becomes
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:scale-product}
\tau s\ell<1.
\end{equation}
Thus, after the relative steps are selected, stability fixes only the
product $\tau s$, not the ratio $\tau/s$.  With safety margin
$\delta\in(0,1)$ we set
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:scale-saturation}
\tau s
=
\frac{1-\delta}{\ell}.
\end{equation}

A convenient balance parameter is
\[
q:=\frac{\tau}{s}.
\]
Equations~\eqref{eq:scale-product}--\eqref{eq:scale-saturation}
then give explicitly
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:tau-s-explicit}
\tau
=
\sqrt{
\frac{(1-\delta)q}{\ell}
},
\qquad
s
=
\sqrt{
\frac{1-\delta}{q\ell}
}.
\end{equation}
Thus $q$ determines the primal-versus-dual balance while the product
is fixed by the spectral stability limit.

The value of $q$ does not affect the stability product but can affect
transient convergence.  If no reliable estimate of the relative
primal and dual scales is available, $q$ may be chosen from the
single-channel implementation and retained when changing only the
dyadic channel ratios.  Alternatively, a short logarithmic sweep in
$q$ can be performed without retuning $\rho_\sigma$, since
\eqref{eq:tau-s-explicit} maintains the same stability margin for
every candidate.

\paragraph{Optional channel-by-channel stability allocation.}
The geometric schedule is attractive because it requires only one
parameter, but the exact stability-budget result of
Proposition~\ref{prop:dyadic-budget} also permits an adaptive
schedule.  Suppose $\tau$ and the steps of channels
$0,\ldots,i-1$ have been fixed and define
\[
Q_i
=
\tau^{-1}I
-
B
-
\sum_{j=0}^{i-1}\sigma_jA_j.
\]
The largest step that may be assigned to the next octave is
\[
\sigma_i^{\max}
=
\left[
\lambda_{\max}
\left(
Q_i^{-1/2}A_iQ_i^{-1/2}
\right)
\right]^{-1}.
\]
Thus one may choose
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:adaptive-octave-step}
\sigma_i
=
(1-\delta_i)\sigma_i^{\max}
\end{equation}
with channel-specific safety margins $\delta_i>0$.

This construction uses the actual overlap between the new octave and
the stability budget left by all preceding channels.  It therefore
provides an operator-adaptive alternative to the fixed geometric rule
$\sigma_i\propto2^i$.  The geometric schedule is preferable when a
simple scanner-independent parameterization is desired; the adaptive
schedule is preferable when the cost of estimating the generalized
eigenvalues is acceptable and maximal certified low-frequency
amplification is the goal.

\paragraph{Practical tuning procedure.}
The preceding considerations lead to the following default procedure:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
\item
Choose the largest depth $k$ for which the innermost octave is
represented by at least $m_0$ detector-frequency samples.

\item
Set the relative data-channel schedule to
$r_i=2^i$.  Reduce the base below $2$ only if a more conservative
low-frequency amplification is desired; use a base above $2$ only
after explicitly verifying the resulting weighted normal operator.

\item
Choose the discrepancy allocation independently of the step sizes.
For simulated noiseless data, a controlled total-budget allocation
such as $\varepsilon_i=\varepsilon\sqrt{w_i}$ is appropriate.
For measured data, use band-dependent tolerances matched to filtered
noise energy and inflate only the lowest octaves when additional
near-DC model mismatch is expected.

\item
Fix the relative TV steps $r_d$ and compute
\[
\ell
=
\lambda_{\max}
\left(
\sum_i r_iX^\top R_i^\top R_iX
+
\sum_dr_d\partial_d^\top\partial_d
\right)
\]
by power iteration.

\item
Choose a primal--dual balance $q=\tau/s$ and set
\[
\tau
=
\sqrt{\frac{(1-\delta)q}{\ell}},
\qquad
s
=
\sqrt{\frac{1-\delta}{q\ell}},
\qquad
\sigma_i=sr_i,
\qquad
\sigma_d=sr_d.
\]

\item
Optionally replace the geometric low-octave steps by the
channel-by-channel generalized-eigenvalue ceiling
\eqref{eq:adaptive-octave-step} when a maximally aggressive
operator-adaptive schedule is desired.
\end{enumerate}

This parameterization separates three effects that are otherwise easy
to conflate.  The depth $k$ determines the spectral resolution of the
fidelity decomposition; the tolerances $\{\varepsilon_i\}$ determine
how much discrepancy is permitted in each band and therefore affect
the reconstructed solution; and the steps
$\tau,\{\sigma_i\},\{\sigma_d\}$ determine how rapidly the fixed
optimization problem is traversed.  The critical dyadic schedule
$\rho_\sigma=2$ concerns only the last of these: it is a
stability-efficient way to redistribute dual motion toward the
low-frequency octaves, not a prescription for changing the amount of
low-frequency data agreement required at the solution.
\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

We evaluate the two-channel formulation on 2D limited-angle fan-beam reconstruction with two aims: to examine, on the actual reconstruction operator, the band separation that Remark~\ref{rmk:tight} relies on, and to assess whether amplifying the low-frequency dual step accelerates the convergence of low-frequency image content.

\subsection{Setup}

All reconstructions use a $256\times256$ image on a $10\,\mathrm{cm}$ field of view and a circular fan-beam scan of $25$ views over a $50^\circ$ arc with $1024$ detector bins (source-to-origin $50\,\mathrm{cm}$, source-to-detector $100\,\mathrm{cm}$), with He--Yuan relaxation $\rho=1.75$. The single-channel fidelity filter is the ramp-weighted square-root Hanning window $R[c]$ with $c=4$. The two-channel filters are its exact partition \eqref{eq:Rlo}--\eqref{eq:two-channel-partition}: the low channel uses $c_{\mathrm{lo}}=8$, so that its window is half the width of the single-channel window and concentrated at DC, and the high channel carries the complementary difference. Tolerances are $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{hi}}=\varepsilon$ and $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{lo}}=1.25\,\varepsilon$. The single-channel baseline uses scalar steps saturating $\tau\sigma\|K\|^2=1$. The two-channel runs place $\tau$ and $\sigma_{\mathrm{hi}}$ at the same operator-norm boundary and scale only the low-frequency dual, $\sigma_{\mathrm{lo}}=s\,\sigma_{\mathrm{hi}}$, with $s\in\{1,4,8\}$; here $s=1$ is the matched-tolerance split with no step amplification. We use five phantoms: the breast phantom of the abstract, a modified Shepp--Logan phantom~\citep{shepp1974fourier}, and three analytic anthropomorphic phantoms (a head, a jaw, and a Defrise-type stack of disks), each taken as a 2D slice and peak-normalized so that the sinogram magnitude is comparable across phantoms. Image error is reported as the RMSE against the ground-truth phantom.

\subsection{Stability diagnostic}

The condition $\tau\lambda_{\max}(M)<1$ and the band separation of Remark~\ref{rmk:tight} depend only on the operator and filters, not on the phantom; Table~\ref{tab:stability} summarizes both. The two data-channel normal operators differ in leading eigenvalue by about a factor of five, and their dominant eigenvectors are numerically orthogonal, consistent with the disjoint dominant subspaces assumed in Remark~\ref{rmk:tight}. Writing the certified margin as $\tau\lambda_{\max}(M)=\lambda_{\max}(\widetilde M(s))/\lambda_{\max}(\widetilde M(1))$, where $\widetilde M(s)$ weights the low channel by $s$, the margin stays at the single-channel boundary ($\approx 1$) for $s$ up to roughly $6$, and over this range the low channel contributes essentially no energy to the limiting eigenmode; beyond the crossover, at $s=8$, the low channel governs $\lambda_{\max}(M)$ and the margin exceeds one. Within the certified range the low-frequency step can thus be amplified without reducing $\tau$. We therefore operate at $s=4$, the largest amplification that holds $\tau\lambda_{\max}(M)$ at the single-channel boundary, so that the primal step is taken at its maximal certified value; setting the steps from the conservative worst-channel surrogate of Remark~\ref{rmk:tight} would instead reduce $\tau$ and sacrifice much of the gain.

\subsection{Convergence}

Table~\ref{tab:rmse} reports the image RMSE at iteration $500$ for the single-channel baseline together with the relative reduction of each two-channel setting. The matched-tolerance split without amplification ($s=1$) stays within a few percent of the baseline on all phantoms. The certified setting $s=4$ lowers the RMSE on every phantom at this iteration count, by $9$ to $36\%$, while the uncertified setting $s=8$ adds at most a few further percentage points.

Figure~\ref{fig:conv} indicates that these reductions are not confined to the final iterate: the $s=4$ and $s=8$ curves separate from the single-channel and $s=1$ curves within the first tens of iterations and remain below them, apart from a short interval near iteration $50$ in the oscillatory phase where the curves cross. The size of the reduction varies across phantoms; it is larger for the head and Defrise phantoms than for the more textured jaw, possibly reflecting their greater low-frequency content, though we do not investigate this further. Figures~\ref{fig:ladder-breast}--\ref{fig:ladder-defrise} show the corresponding iteration ladders for all five phantoms. For the Defrise phantom (Figure~\ref{fig:ladder-defrise}) the single-channel iterate retains a low-frequency shading across the disk stack over several hundred iterations, whereas the $s=4$ iterate resolves the stacked disks earlier.

Two comparisons are being made here and they are not of the same kind. The three two-channel settings $s\in\{1,4,8\}$ share the tolerances $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{hi}},\varepsilon_{\mathrm{lo}}$ and therefore the same feasible set and the same saddle point; the step sizes affect only the iterate trajectory, so the differences among them are in convergence speed at a fixed iteration budget and not in the reconstruction reached at convergence. The single-channel baseline, by contrast, is a different constrained problem: \eqref{eq:single} imposes one $\ell_2$ constraint on the full filtered residual whereas \eqref{eq:two} imposes two, one per band, and by \eqref{eq:tolerance-total} the latter is not equivalent to the former for any choice of $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{hi}},\varepsilon_{\mathrm{lo}}$. Part of the single-channel to two-channel difference may therefore reflect a different optimum rather than faster convergence to a common one; Section~\ref{sec:experiments} does not separate the two effects, and the $\varepsilon$ study below bounds only the sensitivity to the tolerances themselves.

\subsection{Sensitivity to the data tolerance}

The tolerances $\varepsilon_c$ enter the model only through the data-constraint set, not through the step-size condition $\tau\lambda_{\max}(M)<1$; that condition depends on $K$ and the step sizes alone. Changing $\varepsilon$ therefore moves the saddle point but does not affect convergence, provided the constraints remain feasible. Table~\ref{tab:eps} reports a preliminary check on the breast phantom with the step sizes fixed at the maximal certified amplification $s=4$. Loosening the global tolerance for single-channel reconstruction ($\varepsilon:1.0\to1.25$, in units of the nominal $\varepsilon$) and loosening the low-band tolerance for the two-channel method ($\varepsilon_{\mathrm{lo}}:1.0\to1.25$) each change the iteration-$500$ RMSE by at most about one percentage point, against the $\sim\!20\%$ reduction obtained from the step amplification itself. In the range tested the reconstruction is thus insensitive to $\varepsilon$, and the acceleration is attributable to the certified low-frequency step amplification rather than to the tolerance. A wider $\varepsilon$ sweep is left to future work.
